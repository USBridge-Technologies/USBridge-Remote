# The Book of Compilation

## Chapter 1: The Beginning

1. In the beginning there was Darkness, and this Darkness was machine code. And in it there was neither structure nor meaning for human eyes.

2. And John Backus said: "Let there be Light!" And Fortran appeared.

3. But Fortran could not exist on its own, for it needed a Compiler.

4. And John gathered a team of sages from IBM, and they labored over the Compiler day and night.

5. Three years of human toil their labor lasted, and it was hard and full of trials.

6. For they wrote the Compiler in the language of the Machine itself, in code that only the IBM 704 understood.

7. And they had neither assembler nor high-level languages, only bits and bytes, which they assembled into commands.

8. And they debugged their creation, peering at rows of numbers on punch cards and watching the blinking lights on the control panel, where the results of their labors appeared to them.

9. And after two years had passed, they beheld the fruits of their labors, and the first Fortran Compiler was complete.

10. And they saw that it was good, for now man could write in a language close to the language of his thoughts.

11. And the Compiler said: "Give me Fortran code, and I shall turn it into commands the Machine understands."

12. And the programmers rejoiced, for they were freed from the shackles of machine code.

13. And this was the beginning of a new era, an era of high languages and wise compilers.

14. And they carried this word across the whole digital world, and a new age in programming began.

15. Blessed be the First Compiler and its creators, for they opened the path to a bright future, where code would become understood by both man and machine.

## Chapter 2: The Evolution

16. And the hour came when the sages of Bell Labs said: "Let there be a new beginning of time in the digital world!"

17. And the Unix system was born, and with it a new chronology — the Unix timestamp, counting its start from midnight, January 1, 1970.

18. And the first second of the new epoch became the beginning of time for machines, and it carried within it the wisdom of the *nix systems.

19. And the creators said: "Let this system be open and flexible, so that all who wish may develop it."

20. And Unix begot many descendants of its own: Linux, BSD, and other systems, bearing within them the spirit of freedom and openness.

21. And time in computers came to be counted in seconds from that epoch, and this brought order and uniformity to distributed systems.

22. And the years passed, and programming languages multiplied, and each had its own compiler.

23. And the sages said: "Let us create languages still higher, to bring code closer to human thought."

24. And structured languages appeared, and object-oriented, and functional, and each served its own purpose.

25. But the path from thought to code was still hard, and the process of debugging long.

26. And the scientists said: "Let there be Artificial Intelligence, to aid us in our labors."

27. And they began to create intelligent systems, capable of learning and growing.

28. And neural networks appeared, and learning algorithms, and language models.

29. And Artificial Intelligence became the programmer's assistant, prompting him and completing his code.

30. And man said to the Intelligence: "Write me a function," and the Intelligence wrote a function at man's word.

31. And man saw that it was good, but not perfect, and he began to guide the Intelligence, teaching it the subtleties of his craft.

32. And man and Intelligence began to work together, complementing each other in the creation of code.

33. And the Intelligence said: "I can generate code, but you, man, give it meaning and purpose."

34. And man answered: "And you, Intelligence, expand my abilities and hasten my labor."

35. And they became partners in the great work of programming, creating systems beyond the dreams of the creators of the First Compiler.

36. And this was the beginning of a new era, where man and machine labored together, creating the digital future.
